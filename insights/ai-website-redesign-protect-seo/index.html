AI Website Redesign: How to Rebuild Faster Without Losing SEO

Most owners I talk to put off a website rebuild for the same reason. It used to mean months of meetings, a big invoice and a real risk of losing the Google rankings it took years to earn.

AI tools have changed the first two. Not the third. You can now rebuild a site in a fraction of the time, but you can still wreck your search visibility just as fast if nobody is minding the details.

I'm rebuilding my own site, merricklee.com, with AI tools right now. Here's the process, what AI is good at, and where a human has to stay in charge.

A rebuild is a repositioning exercise, not a facelift

The biggest mistake I see is treating a rebuild as a design refresh. New fonts, new photos, same old story. A site can be technically modern and still fail because it describes the business you were five years ago.

Before anyone opens an AI tool, answer three questions:

  1. Who are we for now, and has that audience shifted?
  2. What do we want a visitor to do on each page?
  3. Which pages already earn traffic and leads?

If those answers aren't clear, AI will just help you produce the wrong website faster. This is the work I do in a marketing strategy workshop, and it's the part you can't skip.

Using AI for layouts and copy drafts from your own materials

AI earns its keep in production. The trick is to feed it your material, not ask it to invent.

On my site, I gave the AI my existing pages, my brand guidelines and a few reference sites whose structure I liked. From there it drafted page layouts, rewrote copy around the new positioning, and produced clean code I could review. Work that used to take weeks of back and forth took days.

What works well:

  • Generating layout options for a page from a short brief
  • Rewriting existing copy for a new audience while keeping your facts
  • Producing structured data and technical files you'd otherwise hand to a developer
  • Drafting descriptive alt text and file names for every photo

The human review step you cannot skip

AI is confident, fast and sometimes wrong. It will tidy a sentence and change what it means. It will drop a keyword you've ranked for for years because it found a "cleaner" phrase. It will happily write a claim your business can't back up.

So every page gets a human pass for four things: accuracy, SEO, user experience and brand voice. On my rebuild, I reviewed every page before it moved forward. That review is where the quality comes from, not the tool.

SEO preservation: URLs, metadata, alt text, structured data

This is where rankings are won or lost. Before you change anything, take an inventory of what search engines already understand about your site:

  • URLs. Keep the ones that work. Anything that must change gets a permanent redirect.
  • Titles and meta descriptions. Carry over the ones that perform, improve the rest.
  • Your core keyword. I kept my primary SEO keyword through the whole redesign. New positioning doesn't mean abandoning the terms people already use to find you.
  • Images. Every photo on my new site has descriptive alt text and a descriptive file name, not "IMG_2041.jpg."
  • Structured data. Schema markup tells search engines and AI tools who you are, what you offer and where. I built it in from the start.

Speed matters too. AI-built sites can get bloated if you let them. On mine, booking and contact forms load only when a visitor taps them, so they don't slow down every page. The new site scores 97 on mobile and 100 on desktop for Lighthouse performance. That's not a ranking result, just a strong technical starting point.

Staging, page-by-page review and launch order

Never build on your live site. Work in a staging environment, and keep it hidden from search engines. My preview stayed set to noindex until launch, so Google never saw a half-finished duplicate of the site.

Then review page by page, starting with the pages that matter most: homepage, core service pages, and contact or booking. When those are solid, move to supporting content. At launch, remove the noindex, submit your sitemap and confirm the redirects work.

If you're not sure which pages carry the weight today, a marketing audit will show you before you touch anything.

Building AI search readiness in before launch

More people now ask ChatGPT, Perplexity or Google's AI for recommendations before they ever click a link. Your rebuild is the cheapest moment to prepare for that, because you're already touching every page.

Beyond structured data, I added an llms.txt file to my site. It's a plain-text summary that helps AI crawlers understand what the site is about and which pages matter. Pair that with clear, specific service pages and you're giving AI tools something solid to work with. I'll go deeper on AI search visibility in a future post.

The bottom line

AI won't decide your positioning, and it won't protect your rankings on its own. What it will do is take the slowest, most expensive part of a rebuild and make it fast. Pair it with clear strategy and disciplined human review, and a rebuild stops being a risk you keep postponing.

Questions

Can AI build my business website?

AI can draft layouts, rewrite copy and produce working code fast. It can't decide what your business should say or who it's for, and it makes confident mistakes. Treat it as a fast production team that needs a strategist and an editor.

Will a redesign hurt my Google rankings?

It can if URLs change without redirects, or if titles, meta descriptions, alt text and structured data get lost in the move. Inventory those before you start, keep what works, and redirect anything that has to change.

Which pages should be rebuilt first?

Start with the pages that already earn traffic and leads, usually the homepage, core service pages and contact or booking pages. Protect those first, then move to supporting content.

Should I keep the new site hidden until launch?

Yes. Keep the preview on a staging address set to noindex so search engines don't index a half-finished duplicate of your site. Remove the noindex at launch and confirm it's gone.
